About

I am a Research Scientist at Stanford University in the group of Zhenan Bao in Chemical Engineering, where I also lead metrology development and support in nano@Stanford, the university's shared nanofabrication and characterization facilities.

My research is organised around the few tens of nanometres at a polymer interface, where blends, composites and stretchable devices succeed or fail. I build the measurement methods that resolve chemistry, mechanics and transitions at that scale, under the conditions in which materials are used. PRIMA, which heats one phase of a polymer blend with the pulsed laser of an AFM-IR instrument and reads its thermomechanical response there, is the method I am best known for. I also make interfaces with light and solvent whose structure is known in advance, and I work on how a fast measurement can stand in for a slow one, so that characterization keeps pace with materials discovery.

I trained in materials science at the University of Stuttgart and completed my PhD in polymer surface chemistry with Christopher Barner-Kowollik at Queensland University of Technology, before joining Stanford as a DFG Walter Benjamin Fellow.

Beyond the lab

Outside the lab I am a competitive long-distance runner, racing mainly at the marathon distance. Most of my training is long, easy mileage on the roads and trails around the Bay Area, with a few harder sessions each week. This year I am training for the California International Marathon in Sacramento in December.

Every so often I also get talked into ultra-distance trail races. Those are less about speed and more about long days out on the trails with friends. I also coach other runners and enjoy pacing friends to their own race goals.
