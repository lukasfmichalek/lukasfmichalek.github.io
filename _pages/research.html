Research

How chemistry and processing shape polymers at the nanoscale.

Polymers are rarely uniform. Their properties depend on how they were synthesized, attached to a surface or processed into a film, and the differences that matter often sit at length scales of tens of nanometers. My work connects polymer chemistry to those nanoscale structures and to the function of real materials, and develops the measurements needed to make that connection quantitative.

01

Polymer chemistry at surfaces and interfaces

How do you control what a polymer does at a surface, and how do you know you succeeded?

My PhD work quantified the limits of attaching polymers to surfaces, including how chain length and solvent quality set the achievable grafting density, and mapped photochemical reactivity across surfaces with spatial resolution. This led to light-controlled systems: wavelength-selective photoresists that form interpenetrating networks from a single resin, light-gated polymer brushes, and photolyzable brushes for subtractive 3D structuring with water and light.

The same chemistry now feeds applied interface problems, from bioinspired coatings that remove metals and fluorinated compounds from water to directed functionalization of polymer semiconductor interfaces. Parts of this work are covered by patents on light-gated adhesives and active stormwater treatment.

  • Polymer on Top, Adv. Mater. 2018
  • Mapping Photochemical Reactivity Profiles on Surfaces, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2020
  • Interpenetrating Polymer Networks from a Single Photoresist, Adv. Funct. Mater. 2020
  • Directed Functionalization of Polymer Semiconductor Interfaces, ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2026

02

Processing, structure and properties of soft electronic and energy materials

Why does the same formulation work in one film and fail in the next?

In stretchable electronics and batteries, performance is often set by nanoscale morphology and buried interfaces that bulk measurements average away. With the Bao Group and collaborators, I study how molecular weight, temperature and solution-state aggregation control phase separation in blends of conjugated polymers and elastomers, and how that morphology carries through to intrinsically stretchable transistors, circuits and biosensors.

The same approach extends to energy materials, including interphase formation at lithium-metal electrodes and self-segregated polymer electrolytes for solid-state batteries.

  • Environmentally Stable and Stretchable Polymer Electronics, Nat. Nanotechnol. 2023
  • Nanoconfined Aggregation in Conjugated Polymer and Elastomer Blends, PNAS 2026
  • Intrinsically Stretchable Complementary Circuits, Nat. Electron. 2026
  • Solid-Electrolyte Interphase at Li-Metal Potential, Nat. Chem. 2025

03

Nanomechanics and thermomechanics of soft matter

What is the modulus, loss tangent or glass transition of a single phase that is only tens of nanometers wide?

Soft and viscoelastic materials break many of the assumptions behind standard contact mechanics, so quantitative nanomechanics depends on careful choices of mode, calibration and reporting. I work on quantitative mapping of modulus and viscoelastic response in thin films, blends and polymer networks, on resolving subsurface structure, and on measurements under strain and temperature, which are the conditions in which soft devices typically fail.

Our STAR Protocols guide sets out mode selection, calibration and data-reporting practice for nanomechanical AFM of soft matter.

  • A Guide for Nanomechanical Characterization of Soft Matter via AFM, STAR Protoc. 2025
  • Autonomous Alignment and Healing in Multilayer Soft Electronics, Science 2023
  • Localized Thermomechanical Measurements with AFM-IR, Small 2026

04

Measurement physics and instrument development

What else can an existing instrument measure if you change how you drive it?

Many of the capabilities soft materials need are latent in commercial instruments. PRIMA (Photothermal Resonant Infrared Mechanical Analysis) uses chemistry-selective infrared heating in AFM-IR to probe thermomechanical transitions of individual phases in polymer blends, rather than heating the whole sample. PRIMA-nDMA adds nanoscale dynamic mechanical analysis to resolve the viscoelastic response of each phase, in collaboration with NIST, Bruker and the University of Maryland.

Current work focuses on quantitative temperature calibration and modeling, and on faster surrogate protocols. The aim is to generate large, realistic datasets of the kind polymer machine learning currently lacks, including imperfect and failed materials. This builds on earlier development of a laser-coupled AFM for dynamic polymer networks at QUT.

  • PRIMA: Localized Thermomechanical Measurements of Polymers and Blends, Small 2026
  • Spatially-Resolved Viscoelastic Response with AFM-IR, ChemRxiv 2026

Approach

The four threads share one goal: measuring polymers at the length scales where they are heterogeneous, and under the conditions in which they are processed and used. The next steps are hyphenated AFM platforms that combine thermal, mechanical and electrical modes at the same location, measurements under strain, and open datasets that connect experimental polymer science to data-driven design.

Atomic force microscopy

PeakForce QNM, multifrequency and force spectroscopy for quantitative nanomechanics.

AFM-IR and nanoscale IR

Contact and resonance-enhanced AFM-IR, PRIMA and PRIMA-nDMA for correlated chemistry and mechanics.

Surface and polymer chemistry

Grafting, photochemistry and photoresists, with surface analysis and neutron reflectometry.

Data and modeling

Python analysis pipelines, calibration and artifact correction, and numerical transport models.
