        <span class="theme-n">01</span>
        <h2 class="mt-0">Nanoscale Metrology for Soft &amp; Polymeric Materials</h2>
        <div class="theme-sub">Why it matters</div>
        <p>Soft and polymeric materials are mechanically and chemically heterogeneous at the nanoscale, but most characterization tools were designed for hard, crystalline solids. Applied uncritically, they produce artifacts rather than insight.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">What we study</div>
        <p>Nanomechanical mapping of polymer thin films, interpenetrating networks, and blends — resolving viscoelastic contrast and chemical composition down to tens of nanometers.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">How we probe it</div>
        <p>Multi-frequency and PeakForce AFM for quantitative nanomechanical mapping, and AFM-IR for correlated chemical identification, following the practices set out in our <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12166430/">STAR Protocols guide to nanomechanical AFM</a>.</p>

        <span class="theme-n">02</span>
        <h2 class="mt-0">Soft &amp; Stretchable Electronic Materials</h2>
        <div class="theme-sub">Why it matters</div>
        <p>Stretchable transistors, organic semiconductors, and skin-like biosensors fail or perform at their interfaces. Understanding why requires resolving chemistry, mechanics, and transport at the same nanoscale location.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">What we study</div>
        <p>Intrinsically stretchable polymer transistors and complementary circuits, photo-patternable semiconductors, and diode-connected organic field-effect transistors for skin-like biosensing, developed with the Bao Group.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">How we probe it</div>
        <p>AFM-IR and nanomechanical mapping correlated with device performance, connecting processing history to local structure and, in turn, to function.</p>

        <span class="theme-n">03</span>
        <h2 class="mt-0">Polymer Blends, Interfaces &amp; Phase Behavior</h2>
        <div class="theme-sub">Why it matters</div>
        <p>Blend morphology and phase separation set the mechanical and electronic properties of soft-electronic materials, but the relevant length scales are far below what conventional imaging resolves.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">What we study</div>
        <p>Conjugated polymer:elastomer blends, nanoconfined aggregation, and temperature-controlled phase separation — work carried out with the Bao Group, Salleo Group, and collaborators at Penn State.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">How we probe it</div>
        <p>Correlated AFM nanomechanics with scattering and microscopy to connect nanoscale structure to macroscopic blend behavior.</p>

      <div class="theme-body">
        <span class="theme-n">04</span>
        <h2 class="mt-0">Energy Materials</h2>
        <div class="theme-sub">Why it matters</div>
        <p>Battery performance and lifetime are governed by nanoscale interfaces — the solid-electrolyte interphase, electrode coatings, and thin polymer layers — that are difficult to characterize without disturbing them.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">What we study</div>
        <p>Interphase formation at lithium-metal electrodes and polymer electrolyte layers for lithium-metal and lithium-sulfur batteries, in collaboration with the Cui Group and Bao Group.</p>
        <div class="theme-sub">How we probe it</div>
        <p>Nanoscale mechanical and chemical mapping of buried and beam-sensitive interfaces, applying the same soft-matter metrology developed for polymer and electronic materials.</p>
